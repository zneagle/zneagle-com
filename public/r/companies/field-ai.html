01Why this company

Field AI's problem isn't just building capable models — it's making robotic intelligence reliable enough to operate in uncertain physical environments. That maps directly onto the systems questions I focus on: interfaces, failure paths, feedback, and what happens when computational outputs become physical consequences. Its Boston R&D work is described as risk-aware, field-ready AI with globally deployed systems feeding rapid iteration.

02Where their work intersects my trajectory

  • Audio engineering — signal paths, routing, control, interfaces, dependencies.
  • Mechanical diagnosis — physical causality and failure isolation.
  • Fabrication and welding — material reality and build execution.
  • Level99 — sensors, monitored controls, Arduino, lighting, mechanisms, computer control.
  • Electrical study — power, protection, theory, safety.

Field AI's own hardware description is explicitly cross-functional — sensing, compute, electrical, mechanical, production, QA, debugging, deployment, service — which is the same surface my background sits across: hardware integration, testing, debugging, QA, field deployment, and cross-functional hardware/software/ML interaction.

Field AI's current Boston openings (Embedded Systems Engineer and Electrical Engineer, Humanoid Robotics) call for professional firmware/BSP, PCB-design, and power-architecture depth I haven't earned yet. I'm not presenting myself as a finished embedded or electrical engineer for those roles — my interest is the technician, integration, test, field-support, and hardware-operations paths where my hands-on systems background can contribute immediately while I build that deeper layer.

04Letter

Field AI letter, 2026-09-17. Written as strategic interest, not an application to the current humanoid-robotics engineering openings.

Read the full letter

Dear Field AI Team,

I have spent years becoming increasingly interested in the same problem from different directions: what happens when information, control, hardware, and uncertainty have to resolve into useful physical behavior.

Audio engineering was the first place I learned to see that problem structurally. A signal moves through sources, transformations, routing decisions, control points, interfaces, dependencies, and feedback. When the final result is wrong, useful troubleshooting means tracing the topology until the observed system diverges from the expected one.

Mechanical diagnosis gave me the physical version of the same lesson. Fabrication taught me that models eventually collide with material reality. Interactive technical work at Level99 put sensors, PLC-monitored elements, DMX, Arduino hardware, programmable LEDs, audio, mechanisms, and computer control into the same operating environment. Electrical study added power, protection, and formal circuit behavior. Software and AI extended the same questions into information, orchestration, and decision systems.

That is why Field AI is technically compelling to me.

Your public description centers on risk-aware, reliable, field-ready robotic AI rather than intelligence that only works under laboratory assumptions. The hardware work is similarly integrated: sensing, compute, power, communications, mechanical packaging, production, QA, debugging, deployment, and servicing all have to converge on systems that function in real environments.

I want to be precise about my current level. I am not presenting myself as qualified today for your Embedded Systems Engineer or Electrical Engineer humanoid positions on the strength of credentials alone. Those roles call for professional depth in areas such as firmware, BSP and kernel work, PCB design, power architecture, high-speed communications, and advanced electrical engineering that I have not earned yet.

What interests me is the environment around that work: hardware integration, build, test, debugging, field deployment, servicing, reliability, and the boundary where software and learned behavior meet physical systems.

I bring real fabrication and welding experience, long-term mechanical troubleshooting, sensor-system exposure, electrical fundamentals, field adaptability, and a signal-path model of systems that has transferred repeatedly across domains. I am trying to deepen that foundation inside robotics rather than relabel it as expertise I do not yet have.

If Field AI has a path where that hands-on, cross-domain foundation is useful — now or as the Boston team grows — I would like to understand it. Thank you for taking the time to consider this, and for whatever we get to discuss at RoboBoston.

Sincerely,

Zachary Neagle

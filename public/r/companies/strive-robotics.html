01What Strive says it builds

Strive Robotics describes its work as “physical agents for manufacturing,” organized around a loop of Understand → Execute → Adapt → Verify.

I read three groups of its public role material: an early-career Robotics Engineer description, a Hardware & Mechatronics internship description, and senior software framing. I am reading them to understand the environment, not applying to them or claiming fit for them.

02Technical environment in the role material

  • Robotics Engineer (early career)Integration of sensors, end-effectors, robots and manufacturing equipment; experiments, failure analysis, repeatability and recovery.
  • Hardware & Mechatronics (internship)Fabrication, electronics, sensors, actuators, wiring and embedded controls; prototyping, testing and debugging.
  • Senior software framingPlanning, execution, verification and recovery, with human review.

Across all three, execution is followed by verification, and failure is followed by recovery.

03Evidenced adjacent experience

  • Mixed-system troubleshootingVenue Technician at Level99: maintained and troubleshot interactive systems combining sensors, PLC-monitored elements, DMX lighting, Arduino hardware, programmable LEDs, audio, mechanical components and computer-based controls.
  • Fabrication and buildMetal fabrication; welding, cutting, fitting and rigging; physical build sequencing.
  • Mechanical diagnosisLong-term automotive and small-engine fault diagnosis and repair.
  • Electrical foundationCompleted Martin Electrical School’s Journeyman One program, May 2026.
  • PACA bounded research demonstrator about action authority, developed with AI assistance under my direction and review. It is a research project, not professional robotics experience.

This is physical-systems experience. None of it is professional robotics-engineering experience, and none of it is relabeled as such here.

04Where their work intersects my trajectory

  • Cross-domain troubleshooting (adjacent). The hardware and mechatronics material lists fabrication, electronics, sensors, actuators and wiring. My fabrication, electrical study and Level99 troubleshooting sit on that same physical surface, as adjacent experience in other settings.
  • An intellectual question (not evidence). The loop’s Execute, Verify and recovery steps overlap with a question PAC explores: what separates whether a machine can perform an action, whether it may do so now, and whether it is safe? That overlap is a question I find interesting. It is not evidence that Strive uses, needs, or would benefit from PAC’s semantics.

PAC is described on its own page, including what it does not establish: /r/research/pac.

05Questions I would like to ask

  • In the Understand → Execute → Adapt → Verify loop, what counts as verification of an executed step, and where does the loop hand off to a person?
  • When a step fails, what does recovery look like in practice: a retry, a return to a known state, or a handoff to a technician?
  • How do experiments and failure analysis at the sensor and end-effector interface feed back into repeatability?
  • Where, if anywhere, would a check on authority for a specific physical action sit in an execution loop, or is that already covered by other parts of the system?
  • Are there technician, integration or test-support paths around the hardware and mechatronics work that suit someone moving from physical systems into robotics?

06Explicit limits

I have no professional robotics-engineering experience. My trajectory is from physical systems into robotics, and it is a deliberate transition.

PAC, Level99, automotive, construction, fabrication and my other experience are not professional robotics experience, and I am not relabeling them as such.

PAC is a research demonstrator. It is not production-ready, safety-certified, or a deployed industrial control system, and the overlap with Strive’s loop is a question, not a claim about Strive.

At Level99 I used PLC monitoring interfaces mainly to observe states and test sensors; software personnel handled ladder logic. I do not claim PLC programming responsibility, production controls engineering, production autonomy engineering, functional-safety expertise, ROS proficiency, embedded-firmware expertise, or distributed-systems correctness claims.

01Why this company

Mitsubishi Electric US Industrial Automation manufactures and sells industrial automation hardware — PLCs, variable frequency drives, servo motors, HMIs, industrial robots, and laser/EDM machines — plus the e-F@ctory smart-factory IoT platform, serving automotive, battery, semiconductor, food and beverage, and other manufacturing sectors [company]. It is one of the largest, most established companies on this roster, with an explicit early-career/new-grad/internship hiring track [company].

02Where their work intersects my trajectory

  • Electrical foundation — Journeyman One, circuit/code/safety fundamentals.
  • Sensors / controls — PLC-monitored systems at Level99.
  • Mechanical/automotive diagnosis — physical fault isolation.
  • Field problem solving — equipment operation, unfamiliar systems.

PLCs, servo drives, and industrial controls are directly in line with my electrical study and PLC-monitoring exposure — closer to my actual coursework than most companies here. Mitsubishi's own careers language points toward early-career, new-graduate, and internship tracks rather than only senior hires, which is a realistic entry point for someone building toward controls/automation work rather than already established in it.

I have no professional industrial-automation, PLC-programming, or servo-drive engineering experience. My PLC exposure is monitoring/observation-level, from an interactive venue, not commissioning or programming controls systems. I'm asking about early-career, technician, or support-tier automation roles, not senior applications-engineering positions.

Dear Mitsubishi Electric Automation Team,

Of the companies I'm hoping to meet at RoboBoston, yours is one of the closest to my actual coursework: PLCs, servo drives, and industrial controls are exactly what I studied electrical theory and code toward, and what I worked adjacent to — at a monitoring level — at an interactive venue.

I don't have professional PLC-programming or servo-drive commissioning experience, and I'm not presenting myself as ready for senior applications-engineering roles. What I noticed is that your hiring language points toward early-career, new-graduate, and internship tracks, not only senior positions.

I'd like to know whether there's an early-career, technician, or support-tier automation role where my electrical foundation and PLC-monitoring background could be a starting point. Thank you for considering this, and for whatever time we get at RoboBoston.

Sincerely,

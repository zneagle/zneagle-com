01Why this company

Fleet Robotics builds submersible autonomous robots that clean and inspect ship hulls — biofouling mitigation, dry bulk cleaning, and coating-integrity inspection — to cut maritime fuel consumption and emissions [company]. It's Boston-based (Charlestown) and listed as a current RoboBoston 2026 hiring company under the name "Bogo Robotics," which links directly to Fleet Robotics' own site [organizer] — I'm treating that as the same company under a display-name variant, not two different companies.

02Where their work intersects my trajectory

  • Mechanical/automotive diagnosis — physical fault isolation.
  • Sensors / controls — sensor and PLC-monitored systems at Level99.
  • Field problem solving — equipment operation, multi-state technical work.
  • Electrical foundation — Journeyman One, circuit/code/safety fundamentals.

A submersible hull-cleaning/inspection robot is, underneath the autonomy, a maintained piece of field equipment that has to survive a marine environment — inspection, coating assessment, and data collection are the parts of that work closest to what I already do. I found a recruitment listing at Fleet Robotics [company] but no specific open role I can name here; I'm asking about field-support, test, or operations paths near the robot's deployment rather than claiming a specific requisition.

I have no marine robotics, underwater systems, or autonomous-vehicle experience, and I'm not presenting myself as ready for a robotics-engineering role at Fleet Robotics. What I can point to is sensor/controls exposure, electrical fundamentals, and a consistent pattern of field diagnosis and equipment support.

04Individualized letter

Fleet Robotics letter, 2026-09-18. Written as strategic interest, not a response to a specific posted role.

Read the full letter

Dear Fleet Robotics Team,

What interests me about hull-cleaning and inspection robots is that they only matter if they keep working in a genuinely hostile environment — biofouling, coating wear, the ocean itself. That's a maintenance and reliability problem as much as an autonomy problem, and it's the part closest to the maintenance and troubleshooting I already do.

My background is sensor and PLC-monitored systems work at an interactive venue, a first-year electrical program, long-term mechanical and automotive diagnosis, and multi-state field/technical work. I don't have marine robotics or underwater-systems experience, and I'm not presenting myself as ready for a robotics-engineering role.

I'd like to know whether there's a field-support, test, or operations path near where Fleet Robotics' hull-cleaning robots are actually deployed and maintained. Thank you for considering this, and for whatever time we get at RoboBoston.

Sincerely,

Zachary Neagle
